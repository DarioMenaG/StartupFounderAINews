## 2. PROBLEMS FOUNDERS ARE SOLVING

### The 5 Most-Funded Problem Categories This Week

1. **AI Infrastructure Gaps** — Chips (MatX raised $500M to challenge Nvidia), cloud compute, data pipelines, and model serving remain massively underfunded relative to demand.

2. **Enterprise Workflow Automation** — Replacing manual, repetitive back-office processes in healthcare (prior auth), legal (contract review), and finance (compliance) with AI agents.

3. **Physical-World Intelligence** — Bridging AI into robotics, autonomous vehicles, agriculture, and construction. Eclipse's $1.3B fund signals this is the next wave.

4. **Energy Transition** — Fusion energy (despite some cracks forming in the funding boom), renewables, grid optimization, and carbon capture.

5. **Developer Productivity** — AI coding assistants, platform engineering, and infrastructure-as-code that let small teams ship at the speed of large ones.

## 4. FOUNDER VISION & STRATEGIC SHIFTS

### What Top Founders Believe in 2026

- **AI is infrastructure, not a feature.** Every serious company uses AI. The differentiator is proprietary data, domain-specific workflows, and deep user understanding — not "we use AI."
- **B2B wins over consumer.** Only ~5% of YC W26 is consumer-facing. The money and durability are in solving business problems.
- **Capital efficiency is king.** The era of "growth at all costs" is dead. Investors want clear product-market fit, measured traction, and a path to profitability.
- **Solopreneurship is legitimate.** AI tools enable one person to do the work of a 10-person team. LinkedIn reports AI is "turning workers into founders."
- **Physical-world problems are the frontier.** Pure software is crowded. Startups tackling robotics, energy, agriculture, and construction are raising the biggest rounds.

## 5. YOUR WEEKLY ACTION PLAN — WHAT TO FOCUS ON

### This Week's Priority: Define Your Unfair Advantage

Given the current landscape, here are your top action items:

#### Immediate Actions (This Week)
1. **Pick your domain, not your technology.** AI is table stakes. Choose an industry where you have (or can develop) deep expertise. The best-funded startups in YC W26 are AI-native but industry-specific.
2. **Set up your founder tech stack.** Install Cursor, sign up for Claude/ChatGPT, deploy on Vercel + Supabase. Get comfortable building MVPs with AI assistance — this is now a core founder skill.
3. **Start talking to potential customers.** Product-market fit is a non-negotiable investor requirement. Begin 5 customer discovery interviews this week in your target sector.

#### Medium-Term (Next 2–4 Weeks)
4. **Build a quick MVP.** Use AI tools to ship a working prototype in under 10 weeks. Focus on one specific workflow pain point for one specific customer segment.
5. **Study the YC W26 batch.** Analyze the 196 companies — identify what they're NOT building. Gaps in the batch often signal overlooked opportunities.
6. **Build in public.** Founders growing their LinkedIn followings in 2026 are using AI to create content. Start documenting your journey — distribution is a moat.

#### Strategic Positioning
7. **Target B2B, not consumer.** The data is overwhelming — B2B startups get funded, consumer startups don't (in this cycle).
8. **Seek structural moats.** Proprietary data, network effects, or regulatory expertise that can't be easily copied.
9. **Consider underserved geographies.** LatAm and Africa present massive opportunities with less competition.
